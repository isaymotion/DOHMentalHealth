# MindPlan Release 14 — Pass 4
## Practice-question bank for High-Yield Revision

**Status:** Integrated into MindPlan v2.14.0 as a dedicated 30-item formative assessment. These questions are not a validated examination. The items below are grounded in the source-mapped notes and Pass 2–3 content. They are formative learning checks, not a validated examination.

**Primary source:** Philippine Council for Mental Health, *National Mental Health Strategic Plan 2019–2023* (supplied PDF). Source anchors refer to the existing page-mapped notes. Check the source PDF for formal citation and policy interpretation.

**Item-writing rules**
- Keep source-supported recall separate from learning-interpretation questions.
- Avoid scoring details from scanned/unverified IRR text, unverified M&E/scorecard row alignments, or uncertain framework diagram relationships.
- Historical targets and proposed budgets must never be framed as proof of current performance or actual expenditure.
- Feedback should explain why the answer is best, not merely reveal a letter.

## A. Single-best-answer questions

### Q1. Vision
**Question:** Which phrase is identified as the vision of the National Mental Health Strategic Plan 2019–2023?

A. Mental health and wellbeing for all Filipinos  
B. Mental health conditions are identified, treated and prevented  
C. Four pillars and five outcome areas  
D. Information and Research

**Answer:** A  
**Rationale:** “Mental Health and Wellbeing for All Filipinos” is the Plan’s vision. B is Goal 2; C describes parts of the framework; D is one of the four pillars.  
**Source/status:** PDF p. 37; source-supported.

### Q2. Mission
**Question:** Which statement best describes the Plan’s mission?

A. To report only the annual number of mental health service users  
B. To value, promote and protect the basic right of Filipinos to mental health and wellbeing, and provide comprehensive, integrated, accessible and quality programs and services  
C. To replace the Mental Health Act with a strategic plan  
D. To ensure every indicator reaches 100%

**Answer:** B  
**Rationale:** The mission expresses a broad commitment and service direction; it is not a single indicator, a replacement for legislation, or a guarantee of target achievement.  
**Source/status:** PDF p. 37; source-supported, wording summarized from the page-mapped notes.

### Q3. Goal 3
**Question:** Which goal explicitly concerns the ability of persons affected by mental health conditions to exercise the full range of human rights?

A. Goal 1  
B. Goal 2  
C. Goal 3  
D. None of the goals

**Answer:** C  
**Rationale:** Goal 3 explicitly names the full range of human rights. Do not reduce this goal to service availability alone.  
**Source/status:** PDF pp. 37–40; source-supported.

### Q4. Goal vs pillar
**Question:** Which option is one of the Plan’s four strategic pillars, rather than one of its three goals?

A. Mental health and wellbeing is valued, promoted, and protected  
B. Mental health conditions are identified, treated and prevented  
C. Persons affected by mental health conditions exercise the full range of human rights  
D. Information and Research

**Answer:** D  
**Rationale:** Information and Research is a pillar. A–C are the three goals.  
**Source/status:** PDF pp. 37–52; source-supported.

### Q5. Pillar distinction
**Question:** A learner is asked to distinguish the Services pillar from Information and Research. Which is the safest high-level distinction?

A. Services concerns service provision and system development; Information and Research concerns information and evidence functions  
B. Services is a goal, while Information and Research is the Plan’s vision  
C. Information and Research proves that all service targets were achieved  
D. The Plan states that these pillars operate independently

**Answer:** A  
**Rationale:** This is a study distinction, not a claim that the pillars are independent or that information alone proves outcomes.  
**Source/status:** PDF pp. 40–52; learning explanation based on source-supported pillar names.

### Q6. Pillars and outcomes
**Question:** The Plan identifies four pillars and five outcome areas. What conclusion should a learner avoid?

A. The framework has different structural categories  
B. There must be exactly one outcome for each pillar  
C. Outcomes and pillars should not automatically be treated as interchangeable  
D. The relationship should be described only as specifically supported by the source

**Answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Different counts do not establish a one-to-one mapping. Detailed diagram relationships remain verification-gated in the working notes.  
**Source/status:** PDF pp. 40–52; counts source-supported; detailed mapping verification-gated.

### Q7. Indicator vs outcome
**Question:** Which statement best distinguishes an outcome from an indicator?

A. An outcome is a desired result area; an indicator is a measure used to monitor progress  
B. An indicator is proof that the outcome has already happened  
C. The terms always mean the same thing  
D. Outcomes apply only to budgets

**Answer:** A  
**Rationale:** A planned measure tells the reader what would be monitored; it does not by itself establish achievement or causation.  
**Source/status:** Plan monitoring structure; learning explanation.

### Q8. Historical targets
**Question:** A table lists a target for the 2019–2023 planning period. What can be concluded from the target alone?

A. The target was achieved  
B. The target remains the current national performance level  
C. The target was an intended benchmark; achievement requires evidence of observed results  
D. The target proves the intervention caused an outcome

**Answer:** C  
**Rationale:** Targets are planned benchmarks. Observed results and evidence are needed to assess performance; a target alone proves neither achievement nor causation.  
**Source/status:** Historical-period guardrail; learning explanation.

### Q9. Proposed budget
**Question:** Annex 4 lists a proposed budget for the Plan period. Which interpretation is justified by the budget table alone?

A. The full amount was spent  
B. The amount is a proposed historical budget figure, not proof of actual expenditure  
C. All funded activities achieved their intended outcomes  
D. The amount is the current annual budget

**Answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Proposed budget allocations must not be presented as actual spending or as evidence of impact.  
**Source/status:** PDF p. 114; proposed figures verified in Release 13 Pass 5.

### Q10. Scorecard legend
**Question:** In the Plan’s scorecard legend, what does “No performance data available” mean?

A. Performance is zero  
B. The target was fully achieved  
C. Performance data are unavailable; the label is not itself a score of zero  
D. The indicator is not applicable in every context

**Answer:** C  
**Rationale:** Missing performance data and zero performance are not equivalent. The legend separately includes “not applicable for monitored period.”  
**Source/status:** Release 13 Pass 5 visual verification; source-supported.

### Q11. Policy instrument
**Question:** Which description best distinguishes the Mental Health Act from the National Mental Health Strategic Plan 2019–2023?

A. They are interchangeable names for the same document  
B. The Act is a statutory legal framework; the Plan organizes a time-bounded strategy and intended implementation/monitoring  
C. The Plan is a patient-level clinical guideline  
D. The Plan automatically proves legal compliance

**Answer:** B  
**Rationale:** The Act, its IRR, and the time-bounded Strategic Plan have different roles. This is a high-level learning distinction, not legal advice.  
**Source/status:** Annex 1 and Plan framework; high-level roles source-supported in the notes.

### Q12. IRR
**Question:** A question writer wants to test a specific numbered rule from Annex 2, but the relevant scanned page has not been transcribed and verified. What is the best action?

A. Infer the rule from the section heading  
B. Write the item from memory and score it as definitive  
C. Mark the item verification-gated until the source page is visually checked  
D. Replace the rule with a general clinical recommendation

**Answer:** C  
**Rationale:** The source-quality workflow requires exact legal details to be checked before they become scored recall items.  
**Source/status:** Annex 2; verification-gated workflow rule.

### Q13. Guiding approach
**Question:** Which approach is most directly concerned with placing rights and freedoms of people requiring mental-health services at the center of care and policy?

A. Rights-based approach  
B. Information and Research pillar  
C. Proposed budget  
D. Scorecard legend

**Answer:** A  
**Rationale:** The Plan names a rights-based approach. The short description is a learning cue rather than a verbatim formal definition.  
**Source/status:** PDF pp. 40–52; approach name source-supported, cue is learning explanation.

### Q14. Recovery approach
**Question:** Which statement is the best learning cue for the recovery approach as presented in the revision notes?

A. Recovery is equivalent to one clinical endpoint for every person  
B. The person affected has a central role in defining problems and meaningful outcomes

**Source/status:** PDF pp. 40–52; learning explanation; verify source page image before testing exact definitions.

### Q15. Plan timeframe
**Question:** What is the safest way to describe a statistic or target taken from the *National Mental Health Strategic Plan 2019–2023* when discussing the present day?

A. Present it as current without qualification  
B. Label it as historical/source-period information unless current evidence independently updates it

**Answer:** B  
**Rationale:** The source covers a defined historical planning period. Current status requires current evidence.  
**Source/status:** Plan title and source-period guardrail; source-supported.

## C. Short-answer and oral-recall prompts

These prompts are intended for self-explanation or facilitator use. They should not be automatically graded as exact-match responses.

### Q21. Explain the strategic hierarchy
**Prompt:** In your own words, distinguish the vision, mission, goals, pillars, outcomes, activities/outputs and indicators.  
**Model outline:** Vision = desired future; mission = broad commitment and direction; goals = major changes sought; pillars = broad strategic domains; outcomes = intended result areas; activities/outputs = planned actions/deliverables; indicators = measures used to monitor progress. Do not claim a one-to-one pillar/outcome map.  
**Source/status:** PDF pp. 37–52; partly learning explanation.

### Q22. Explain the three goals without collapsing them
**Prompt:** State the three goals and give one sentence on how their emphases differ.  
**Model outline:** Goal 1: mental health and wellbeing is valued, promoted and protected. Goal 2: mental health conditions are identified, treated and prevented. Goal 3: persons affected by mental health conditions can exercise the full range of human rights. The emphases are societal promotion/protection, identification/treatment/prevention, and human rights.  
**Source/status:** PDF p. 37; source-supported.

### Q23. Interpret a target
**Prompt:** A plan specifies a target and an indicator, but no observed result is supplied. What can you conclude, and what remains unknown?  
**Model outline:** The target states an intended benchmark and the indicator describes how progress would be measured. Without observed results and appropriate evidence, achievement, present-day status and causation remain unknown.  
**Source/status:** Learning explanation based on the Plan’s monitoring structure.

### Q24. Explain the budget caveat
**Prompt:** How should a resident present the Plan’s Annex 4 budget figures in a teaching session?  
**Model outline:** Label them as proposed budget figures for the 2019–2023 Plan period. Do not describe them as actual expenditures, current appropriations or proof of achieved outcomes without separate evidence.  
**Source/status:** PDF p. 114; figures visually verified in Release 13 Pass 5.

### Q25. Choose the right source
**Prompt:** A learner needs to answer (a) what the law establishes, (b) how the law is operationalized, and (c) how the 2019–2023 strategy organized planned work and monitoring. Which source should they consult for each?  
**Model outline:** (a) Republic Act No. 11036; (b) its Implementing Rules and Regulations; (c) the National Mental Health Strategic Plan 2019–2023 and its relevant M&E material. For legal interpretation, consult the authoritative legal text; detailed IRR rules are verification-gated in this content set.  
**Source/status:** High-level roles source-supported in the page-mapped notes.

## D. Scenario-based application

### Q26. “The target was met” claim
**Scenario:** A slide shows a 2019–2023 target. A presenter says, “This proves the target was achieved and remains true today.”  
**Question:** Identify two unsupported leaps and give a safer replacement statement.  
**Model outline:** The target alone does not prove achievement; a historical target does not establish present-day status. Safer: “The Plan set this target for 2019–2023; achievement and current status require separate observed data.”  
**Source/status:** Learning explanation; historical-period guardrail.

### Q27. Four pillars, five outcomes
**Scenario:** A draft infographic pairs each of four pillars with exactly one outcome and leaves the fifth outcome unpaired.  
**Question:** What is the source-quality concern?  
**Model outline:** The Plan identifies four pillars and five outcome areas, but the count alone does not support a one-to-one map. The diagram/table relationship must be checked against the source before the graphic is presented as factual.  
**Source/status:** Counts source-supported; detailed mapping verification-gated.

### Q28. Missing scorecard data
**Scenario:** A scorecard cell says “No performance data available.” A reviewer marks it as 0% and concludes the agency failed.  
**Question:** Is that interpretation supported? Explain.  
**Model outline:** No. The legend treats unavailable data as a distinct category; it is not automatically a zero score. The reviewer should report the data limitation and avoid asserting performance without evidence.  
**Source/status:** Release 13 Pass 5 scorecard legend verified.

### Q29. Proposed funds and actual outcomes
**Scenario:** A report cites the proposed Promotion budget of PHP 32,400,000 and states that this amount was spent and successfully improved population mental health.  
**Question:** Which parts require additional evidence?  
**Model outline:** The source notes identify PHP 32,400,000 as a proposed historical budget figure. Actual expenditure requires financial records; outcome improvement requires appropriate outcome evidence. Neither follows from the budget allocation alone.  
**Source/status:** PDF p. 114; proposed figures visually verified in Release 13 Pass 5.

### Q30. Exact legal detail requested
**Scenario:** During oral exams, a candidate is asked to quote a specific IRR section that is not yet verified in the study notes.  
**Question:** What is the academically responsible response for this content set?  
**Model outline:** Do not guess or fabricate a quotation. Identify that the exact provision must be checked in the authoritative IRR text. This study set does not score detailed IRR claims until page-level verification is complete.  
**Source/status:** Annex 2; verification-gated.
