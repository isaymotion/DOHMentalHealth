## Topic 1 — What the Plan is and why it was developed
**Priority:** Core | **Suggested review:** 1 minute | **Source:** Executive Summary, printed p. 1 / PDF p. 15; source overview PDF pp. 3–5.

### One-minute summary
The *National Mental Health Strategic Plan 2019–2023* is the Philippine Council for Mental Health's time-bounded strategic implementation plan developed in the context of Republic Act No. 11036, the Mental Health Act, and its Implementing Rules and Regulations. It sets out the Council's vision, mission, goals, outcomes, outputs, activities, targets, responsible agencies/sectors, indicators and means of verification for the first five years of implementation. It was intended to guide national and subnational/local counterparts and coordinate multi-agency and multi-sector action.

### Remember
- The period is **2019–2023**.
- It is a **strategic implementation plan**, not a patient-level diagnostic/treatment guideline.
- A planned target is not evidence that it was achieved.
- A source-period statistic is not automatically a current estimate.

### Rapid-recall card
**Front:** What does the Plan provide beyond broad goals?

**Back:** Outputs, key activities, targets for 2019–2023, responsible agencies/sectors, indicators and modes of verification for monitoring and evaluation.

**Status:** Source-supported. **Locator:** printed p. 1 / PDF p. 15.

## Topic 2 — Vision, mission and three goals
**Priority:** Core | **Suggested review:** 1 minute | **Source:** printed p. 23 / PDF p. 37; goal indicator tables printed pp. 24–26 / PDF pp. 38–40.

### Exact vision
> Mental Health and Wellbeing for All Filipinos

### Mission — source wording
> Value, promote and protect the basic right of all Filipinos to Mental Health and wellbeing and provide comprehensive, integrated, accessible and quality Mental Health programs and services

### Three goals — source wording
1. **Mental health and wellbeing is valued, promoted, and protected.**
2. **Mental health conditions are identified, treated and prevented.**
3. **Persons affected by mental health conditions are able to exercise the full range of human rights.**

### Learning explanation
The goals express three complementary directions: valuing/promoting/protecting mental health; identifying, treating and preventing conditions; and enabling people affected by mental-health conditions to exercise human rights. Do not collapse these into the four pillars or five outcomes.

### Rapid-recall card
**Front:** State the three goals in order.

**Back:** (1) Mental health and wellbeing is valued, promoted, and protected. (2) Mental health conditions are identified, treated and prevented. (3) Persons affected by mental health conditions are able to exercise the full range of human rights.

**Status:** Source-supported. Preserve the wording and punctuation when exact recall is the task. **Locator:** PDF p. 37.

## Topic 3 — Strategic framework and four pillars
**Priority:** Core + conceptual | **Suggested review:** 2 minutes | **Source:** printed pp. 20–26 / PDF pp. 34–40; framework/outcome discussion continues through PDF p. 52.

### The four pillars
- **Promotion and Prevention**
- **Leadership and Governance**
- **Services**
- **Information and Research**

The Plan presents a phased, multi-year approach and draws on a staging approach, a biopsychosocial model, a balanced-care model, a rights-based approach and a recovery approach.

### Distinguish the concepts
- **Staging approach:** considers that needs and experiences may change across stages, from no symptoms through distress and defined syndromes to recurrence/persistence or treatment resistance.
- **Biopsychosocial model:** considers interacting biological, developmental and environmental factors across the life course.
- **Balanced care:** considers different service platforms, including community, primary, secondary and tertiary care, alongside community and intersectoral action.
- **Rights-based approach:** foregrounds rights and freedoms of people requiring mental-health services.
- **Recovery approach:** centers the person affected in defining their problems and what a successful outcome may mean.

### Learning explanation
A useful memory aid is: **staging = changing needs; biopsychosocial = interacting determinants; balanced care = mix of platforms; rights-based = rights and safeguards; recovery = person-defined goals.** This is a teaching aid, not wording quoted from the Plan.

### Framework caution
The Plan describes **four pillars and five outcomes**. Do not assume there is one outcome per pillar. The framework diagram is a strategy map of intended relationships; it does not prove that activities caused outcomes or that planned results were achieved.

### Rapid-recall card
**Front:** Name the four pillars and explain why “four pillars = four outcomes” is incorrect.

**Back:** Promotion and Prevention; Leadership and Governance; Services; Information and Research. The Plan identifies five outcomes across the four pillars, so a one-to-one mapping is not supported.

**Status:** Pillar names source-supported; exact spatial relationships in the diagram remain verification-gated. **Locator:** PDF pp. 40–52.

## Topic 4 — Policy context and Mental Health Act mandate
**Priority:** Core orientation; section-level legal detail gated | **Suggested review:** 1–2 minutes | **Source:** Executive Summary, printed p. 1 / PDF p. 15; Annex 1 PDF pp. 53–73.

### What the Plan reports
- RA No. 11036, the Mental Health Act, was signed on **20 June 2018** and took effect on **5 July 2018**.
- The Plan reports that the Act's IRR was signed on **22 January 2019**.
- The Executive Summary describes the PCMH as a policy-making, planning, coordinating and advisory body attached to the Department of Health.
- The Plan links the Council's work to protection of rights and freedoms and to rational, unified and integrated mental-health services responsive to Filipinos' needs.

### Six objectives listed in the strategic-framework chapter
1. Strengthen effective leadership and governance for mental health.
2. Develop a comprehensive, integrated, effective and efficient national mental-health care system responsive to psychiatric, neurologic and psychosocial needs.
3. Protect the rights and freedoms of people with psychiatric, neurologic and psychosocial health needs.
4. Strengthen mental-health information systems, evidence and research.
5. Integrate mental-health care into basic health services.
6. Integrate mental-health-promoting strategies in educational institutions, workplaces and communities.

### Do not conflate
The six objectives listed in that chapter are not the same thing as the Plan's three goals or four pillars. The Act, its IRR and the strategic plan are distinct documents.

### Rapid-recall card
**Front:** Name the body described in the Executive Summary as the policy-making, planning, coordinating and advisory body attached to DOH.

**Back:** The Philippine Council for Mental Health (PCMH), as described by the Plan.

**Status:** Source-supported for broad orientation. Exact section-by-section legal recall is verification-gated; use the Act itself for legal interpretation. **Locator:** PDF p. 15; Annex 1 PDF pp. 53–73.

## Topic 5 — Historical situation, policy landscape and gaps
**Priority:** Interpretive; historical statistics require date/context | **Suggested review:** 2 minutes | **Source:** printed pp. 2–17 / PDF pp. 16–31.

### One-minute summary
The Plan's situation overview draws on sources available to its authors, including international estimates, Philippine estimates and surveys, and data on mental, neurological and substance-use conditions. Its gaps discussion motivates coordinated action across promotion/prevention, governance, service access, workforce, information and research, and sectors beyond health alone.

### Historical figures explicitly reported in the Plan
- The Plan cites global prevalence estimates of **4.4% for depression** and **3.6% for anxiety disorders**.
- For the Philippines, it reports **3.3% (3,298,652 people) living with depression** and a suicide mortality rate of **3.2 per 100,000**.
- It cites the 2015 Global School-based Student Health Survey for historical findings among students aged 13–17, including **16.8%** reporting a suicide attempt and **11.6%** seriously considering an attempt in the preceding 12 months.

These are **figures reported by the historical Plan**, not current estimates. The source's underlying citations and years should accompany them whenever reused.

### Learning explanation
The gap narrative is a policy rationale, not a contemporary measurement by itself. The Plan's multi-sector framing means the response is not limited to hospital services: it also includes governance, promotion/prevention, schools, workplaces, communities, information and research.

### Rapid-recall card
**Front:** What is the main caution when using statistics from the Plan's situation overview?

**Back:** Attribute them to the Plan and the cited source/year; do not present them as current estimates or as proof of present-day conditions.

**Status:** Broad themes source-supported. Dense chart values, selected tables and the truncated CRPD passage remain verification-gated. **Locator:** PDF pp. 16–31.

## Topic 6 — Plan development and implementation approach
**Priority:** Interpretive | **Suggested review:** 1 minute | **Source:** “The Road to the Strategic Plan 2019–2023,” printed pp. 18–19 / PDF pp. 32–33; framework PDF pp. 34–36.

### One-minute summary
The Plan describes a development and consultation process leading to a phased implementation approach. It recognizes that building capacity from community settings through primary, secondary and tertiary facilities—and coordinating a whole-of-government and whole-of-society response—would take several years. The 2019–2023 Plan was therefore framed as a phased response balancing the Mental Health Act's ambitions with resources available at the time.

### Distinguish roles
- **Consultation participants** contributed to developing the plan.
- **Responsible agencies/sectors** are identified for implementation, monitoring or reporting in the plan's tables.
- These categories may overlap, but they should not be assumed to be identical.

### Rapid-recall card
**Front:** Why does the Plan use a phased approach?

**Back:** The source explains that building systems and capacities across community through tertiary care, while coordinating whole-of-government and whole-of-society action, would take several years and must be balanced with resources available at the time.

**Status:** Source-supported summary. Detailed stakeholder sequencing should be checked against the narrative before a granular sequence quiz is authored. **Locator:** PDF pp. 32–36.

## Topic 7 — Outcomes, indicators and evaluation
**Priority:** Core concepts; individual table rows gated where noted | **Suggested review:** 2 minutes | **Source:** main narrative printed pp. 24–38 / PDF pp. 38–52; Annex 3 PDF pp. 93–110 (page span and detailed rows require table-level verification).

### The results hierarchy
The Plan connects vision and mission to three goals, four pillars and five outcomes, with outputs, activities, indicators, targets, responsible agencies and means of verification used to describe and monitor intended implementation.

### Read indicator tables carefully
Where supplied, distinguish:
- **Indicator:** what is to be measured.
- **Baseline:** reference value and/or year against which change may be assessed.
- **Target:** intended value for a specified period.
- **Observed result:** a value actually measured and reported.
- **Means of verification/data source:** how evidence is obtained or where it comes from.
- **Numerator/denominator or formula:** how a proportion or rate is calculated, when the table defines it.

A target is not a result. A blank, “TBD,” proxy, unavailable value or missing data field must not be silently completed by inference.

### Selected goal-level examples from the main narrative
- Goal 1 indicators include population self-care, help-seeking and attitudes; national government agency implementation of mandates/workplace policy; and local mental-health ordinance implementation.
- Goal 2 includes suicide mortality rate per 100,000 and service use among people with specified mental, neurological and substance-use conditions and epilepsy.
- Goal 3 concerns people affected by mental-health conditions exercising the full range of human rights; some table rows and year-by-year targets remain unverified.

### Rapid-recall card
**Front:** What is the difference between a target and an observed result?

**Back:** A target is intended performance for a specified period; an observed result is a measured/reported value. The target alone does not establish achievement.

**Status:** General indicator literacy source-supported. Goal 3 target cells, detailed Annex 3 rows, exact target-year alignment and some formulas are verification-gated. **Locator:** PDF pp. 38–52; Annex 3 PDF pp. 93 onward.

## Topic 9 — Annexes 2–3: IRR and Monitoring & Evaluation
**Priority:** Verification-gated for legal rules and detailed target rows | **Suggested review:** 1 minute | **Source:** Annex 2 PDF pp. 74–92; Annex 3 begins after Annex 2 (detailed page/row mapping in Release 13 Pass 4–5 documents).

### IRR: what can safely be said
The Plan contains an annex titled *Implementing Rules and Regulations of the Mental Health Act*. Most pages in the supplied PDF do not yield searchable text. A trustworthy rule-by-rule summary therefore requires page-image inspection and transcription, including careful checks of negations, “shall/may” language, deadlines, exceptions and enumerated requirements.

### M&E: how to read the structure
The monitoring and evaluation framework translates the plan's goals into indicators and related fields. Depending on the table, fields may include baseline year/value/source, targets, means of verification, formula, data source, reporting frequency, risks/assumptions, responsible agency and budget/fund source. Fields vary; do not assume every table has every field.

### Rapid-recall card
**Front:** What should a learner do when an M&E table has a blank target cell?

**Back:** Treat the cell as blank or unresolved unless the source explicitly supplies the value elsewhere and that cross-reference is verified. Do not infer the missing target.

**Status:** General reading guidance source-supported; IRR rule-specific questions and detailed target-row questions are verification-gated. **Locator:** IRR PDF pp. 74–92; consult Release 13 Pass 4–5 for the current annex verification ledger.
