## Chapter 6. Structure of the Strategic Plan
**Source locator:** printed pp. 20–21 / PDF pp. 34–35.

### Purpose and period
The Plan covers **2019–2023** and gives overall direction for implementation of the Mental Health Act. It is intended to guide national government and subnational/local counterparts on key strategies and interventions to scale up access to mental health services.

### Six objectives guiding the PCMH
The Plan lists the Mental Health Act objectives as:
1. Strengthen effective leadership and governance for mental health, including formulation, development and implementation of national policies, strategies, programs and regulations.
2. Develop and establish a comprehensive, integrated, effective and efficient national mental health care system responsive to psychiatric, neurologic and psychosocial needs of Filipinos.
3. Protect the rights and freedoms of people with psychiatric, neurologic and psychosocial health needs.
4. Strengthen information systems, evidence and research for mental health.
5. Integrate mental health care into basic health services.
6. Integrate mental-health-promoting strategies in educational institutions, workplaces and communities.

The source says these objectives were consistent with global principles articulated by the Lancet Commission on Global Mental Health and Sustainable Development (October 2018).

### Models and approaches underpinning the Plan
- **Staging approach:** experiences can range from asymptomatic states to nonspecific distress, subsyndromal/subthreshold symptoms, a fully defined syndrome, recurrence or persistence, and treatment resistance. Intervention opportunities differ by stage. The Plan states that health services matter particularly at later stages, while whole-of-government and whole-of-society action is needed to address risks and promote population mental health.
- **Biopsychosocial model:** mental health reflects interactions among environmental, biological and developmental factors across the life course. The source calls for a non-reductionist approach drawing on diverse disciplinary traditions.
- **Balanced care model:** balance delivery platforms—community, primary, secondary and tertiary hospitals—and include community and intersectoral interventions outside the health sector. Source examples include employment opportunities, child-protection services, improved community understanding of mental disorders and services, long-term social care and suicide prevention.
- **Rights-based approach:** the sustainable-development agenda recognizes the right to the highest attainable standard of physical and mental health. The Mental Health Act affirms the basic right of Filipinos to mental health and fundamental rights of people requiring mental-health services.
- **Recovery approach:** centers the person affected in defining their problems and what a successful outcome may be.

**Learning explanation (not a direct quotation):** These approaches complement rather than replace one another: staging describes changing needs over time; biopsychosocial thinking describes interacting determinants; balanced care describes service-platform mix; rights-based and recovery approaches guide how services should treat and involve people.

## Chapter 7. Theory of Change
**Source locator:** printed p. 22 / PDF p. 36. The source diagram should be visually checked when reproducing exact spatial relationships.

The Plan acknowledges that building systems and capacities from community settings through primary, secondary and tertiary facilities, while integrating a whole-of-society and whole-of-government response, would take several years. It therefore presents the 2019–2023 Plan as a phased approach that balances the Mental Health Act’s ambitions with resources then available.

Its stated architecture comprises:
- **Vision:** a distant future for mental health and wellbeing for all Filipinos.
- **Mission:** an ongoing commitment to value, promote and protect the basic right of all Filipinos to mental health, especially those who require services.
- **Three goals:** measured through indicators intended to track progress by 2023.
- **Four pillars and five outcomes:** treated as connected parts of the framework that contribute to the goals.

### Relationships shown in the diagram
The diagram organizes the goals alongside the outcomes and illustrative outputs/actions under four pillars: Promotion, Governance, Services, and Information. It depicts promotion-related work on mental-health literacy and practices; governance-related work on coalitions, policy integration, rights-based care, financing, governing bodies and accountability; service-related work on community-based services, care across levels, intersectoral integration, suicide prevention, care for carers and provider networks; and information-related work on reporting, health-information integration, a research agenda, a research repository and epidemiological data.

**Caution:** The diagram is a strategy map. It does not by itself prove that an activity caused an outcome or that the planned results were achieved.

## Chapter 12. Service Delivery Outcome
**Source locator:** printed pp. 34–36 / PDF pp. 48–50.

The framework describes improved access to mental-health services at all levels of care. The main narrative identifies the following service-development areas:

1. **Community-based mental-health services.** Develop and expand services outside mental hospitals, in connection with the broader community and health-system response.
2. **Mental-health care-provider networks.** Establish networks linking health and non-health settings, all levels of care and comprehensive services.
3. **Strengthen services in primary, secondary and tertiary health care.** Integrate mental health into basic health and social services; establish inpatient mental-health services in DOH-retained hospitals; develop evidence-based standardized modules for psychiatric and neurologic emergencies; train health workers; establish outpatient acute psychiatric units; explore service models such as telepsychiatry; establish psychosocial rehabilitation, reintegration and relapse-prevention programs; and improve access to medication for priority MNS conditions.
4. **Integrate mental-health and wellbeing services in non-health sectors.** Develop evidence-based standardized training for non-mental-health professionals, including barangay health workers, teachers, human-resource officers, police, fire officers and jail officers, contextualized locally. Train providers with initial priority on geographically isolated and disadvantaged areas (GIDA); train lay people in community-based behavioral-emergency intervention using the “Tulong, Alalay, Gabay” module; develop directories of self-help groups; and provide services in workplaces, schools and communities where feasible. The source names DOLE, CSC, DepEd, CHED, TESDA and DOH as agencies that would issue guidelines.
5. **Develop a national suicide-prevention strategy.** The listed actions include policies/guidelines for hotlines and suicide-prevention strategies; emergency-care process flows linking people in crisis to emergency and support services; standardized training for emergency mental-health responders; training first responders, health professionals and volunteers; mainstreaming prevention into public-health education and other programs/settings; and a suicide-surveillance system that includes people with suicidal ideation.
6. **Develop a Care-for-Carers Program.** Include training and debriefing for people exposed to traumatic incidents, such as emergency workers, first responders, women and children protection desk officers, and social workers.

### Priority action: community-based care manual
The source says a DOH Administrative Order / Manual of Procedures on design and implementation of community-based mental-health care was planned for 2020. Its planned coverage included:
- Facility standards at community, primary, secondary and tertiary levels (stepped-care model).
- Service standards covering clinical and psychosocial services, MHPSS, drug treatment and rehabilitation, management of comorbidities, support for survivors of extreme experiences (e.g. disaster or violence), and other social supports such as nutrition, family support, accommodation and hospice care.
- Human-resource standards, including task-sharing guidelines, and training.
- Logistics.
- Algorithms for service-user flow from inpatient mental-health facilities to primary outpatient community-based care.
- Collection/reporting of service statistics, including continuity of care after inpatient discharge and service coverage for people receiving primary care for mental-health conditions or at-risk concerns.

## Chapter 13. Information and Research Outcome
**Source locator:** printed pp. 37–38 / PDF pp. 51–52.

### Outcome and indicator
The source outcome is **“Increased availability, accessibility and utilization of evidence-based Mental Health Data.”** Its outcome indicator is the proportion of LGUs (municipalities and cities) routinely collecting and reporting mental-health data to PCMH. The target table lists **15% for 2020, 30% for 2021, 45% for 2022 and 60% for 2023**, with DOH named as responsible. A note says that by the end of 2020, each of the 81 provinces would have at least three LGUs providing mental-health services and routinely collecting/reporting data to PCMH.

### Outputs and key activities
1. **Report mental-health information from current agency information systems.** The source gives DOLE’s Labor Inspection Management Information System (LIMIS) and DepEd’s Basic Education Information System (BEIS) as examples.
2. **Integrate mental-health information into the national health information system.** Activities include reviewing existing mental-health information systems and resource mapping; convening data users/producers across health and non-health sectors and civil-society organizations to define program-monitoring data needs (examples include suicide as a notifiable disease and hospital fiscal capacity); designing data-flow algorithms including private facilities and non-health sectors; establishing IT infrastructure; issuing joint guidance/manuals for reporting to the national health information system and PCMH Scorecard; training data producers/users in reporting consistent with human-rights conventions; and engaging LGUs in community surveillance of specified conditions, including suicide. The source lists a 2023 priority-action target for integration.
3. **Adopt and institutionalize a national mental-health research agenda.** The source links this to development of a culturally relevant national program incorporating indigenous concepts and practices. It states a plan-period target of at least one research study per region by 2023 in line with research priorities. Activities include collaboration with PCHRD and NCMH on development/implementation and update frequency; dissemination to research institutions; calls for proposals; conducting studies and disseminating results.
4. **Establish a central repository of mental-health research.** Activities include policy directives on submission/access to research reports and data to NCMH; establishing a network of data producers and users to improve links and multisectoral collaboration (including academe and social sciences) for research, operations research and alternative interventions; creating a functional database; and submission of research reports by end users.
5. **Regularly generate updated epidemiological data.** Priority conditions listed include suicide and self-harm, depression, schizophrenia and bipolar disorders, substance abuse, anxiety, epilepsy, dementia, developmental disorders, and people with or at risk of mental-health concerns (e.g. adverse life events and chronic diseases). The source includes convening health/non-health sectors and CSOs to define data for integration into the National Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS) and other relevant national surveys conducted by PSA and other agencies.

**Verification requirement:** PDF p. 52 continues the epidemiological-data activities beyond the visible excerpt. Verify the full page and any continuation before treating the output/activity list as complete.
