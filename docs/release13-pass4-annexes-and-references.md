## 2. Annex 1 — Republic Act No. 11036, Mental Health Act

### What the annex is

The Plan appends the text of **Republic Act No. 11036**, titled *An Act Establishing a National Mental Health Policy for the Purpose of Enhancing the Delivery of Integrated Mental Health Services, Promoting and Protecting the Rights of Persons Utilizing Psychosocial Health Services, Appropriating Funds Therefor and Other Purposes*. The law was approved on 20 June 2018. The text appears in PDF pp. 53–73 (Act internal pages 39–58).

### Opening provisions

- **Section 1 — Short Title:** identifies the law as the “Mental Health Act.”
- **Section 2 — Declaration of Policy:** affirms the basic right of Filipinos to mental health and the fundamental rights of people who require mental-health services. It describes state commitments to value, promote and protect mental health; treat and prevent mental-health conditions; make timely, affordable, high-quality and culturally appropriate care available; keep services free from coercion and accountable to service users; and support full human-rights participation without stigma or discrimination. It also refers to relevant international human-rights instruments and the Magna Carta for Disabled Persons.
- **Section 3 — Objectives:** six listed objectives concern (a) leadership and governance; (b) a comprehensive, integrated and efficient national care system responsive to psychiatric, neurologic and psychosocial needs; (c) rights and freedoms; (d) information, evidence and research; (e) integration into basic health services; and (f) promotion strategies in schools, workplaces and communities.
- **Section 4 — Definitions:** begins the law’s defined terms. The supplied extraction includes addiction, carer, confidentiality, deinstitutionalization, discrimination, drug rehabilitation, impairment or temporary loss of decision-making capacity, informed consent, legal representatives, mental health, mental-health condition, mental-health facility, mental-health professional, mental-health service provider and mental-health service, among others. Use the Act’s wording and the Plan’s definitions section for exact legal phrasing.

### Decision-making capacity and consent — study focus

The text defines impairment or temporary loss of decision-making capacity through a medically determined inability to provide informed consent. The visible definition lists abilities to understand information about the condition; understand consequences of decisions; understand proposed treatment, methods, effects and possible side effects; and communicate voluntary consent or relevant information. The Act’s definition of informed consent emphasizes voluntary agreement after plain-language disclosure of the proposed treatment’s nature, consequences, benefits, risks and available alternatives.

These are study notes, not a substitute for reading the relevant statutory sections in full. Do not compress legal tests into a broader claim than the text supports.

### Organization of the Act

The annex is organized into ten chapters. The source extraction clearly identifies Chapters I–X; chapter headings and section-level coverage should be checked against the page images before a section-numbered legal index is added to the app. At the level appropriate for orientation, the Act covers general provisions and definitions, rights of service users, treatment/consent safeguards, mental-health services and facilities, workforce and capacity building, research and information, responsibilities of government and relevant institutions, prohibited acts and penalties, appropriations, and final provisions. This thematic map is a study-navigation aid; for exact chapter/section names and numbering, consult the original Act pages.

### High-yield review prompts

4. What functional abilities are listed in the definition of impairment or temporary loss of decision-making capacity?

## 4. Annex 3 — Monitoring and Evaluation Plan

### Purpose and structure

Annex 3 converts the Plan’s vision, mission and goals into indicators and a monitoring framework. It includes fields for indicators, baseline year/value/source, targets for 2020–2023 and 2030 where shown, mode of verification, formula, data source, reporting frequency, risks and assumptions, and—in the more detailed tables—responsible agency and budget/fund source. The exact fields vary by table; blank or absent cells must not be filled by inference.

The opening M&E matrix restates the vision as “Mental health and wellbeing for all Filipinos.” Its mission states the intent to value, promote and protect the basic right of all Filipinos to mental health and wellbeing and provide comprehensive, integrated, accessible and quality mental-health programs and services.

### Goal-level indicator examples visible in the extracted matrix

**Goal 1 — Mental health and wellbeing is valued, promoted, and protected**

1. Increased proportion of the population, by age and sex, who practice self-care (examples in the source include stress management, physical activity, and avoidance of alcohol and dangerous drugs), seek mental-health services for psychosocial distress, and believe people with mental disorders should be treated humanely. Research was described as being in the pipeline for 2021, with baseline and targets to be established. The table shows a five-year survey frequency and flags availability/accessibility of community services as an assumption, while poorly implemented policies or lack of services may reinforce stigma and negative perceptions as a risk.
2. Integration of mental-health policies and programs into national and local plans. The extracted table includes an indicator for national government agencies implementing their Mental Health Act mandate and records “10 NGAs (includes NCMH, PHIC)” in the target column. Another indicator concerns implementation of a mental-health workplace policy by national government agencies, with annual milestones displayed as 60%, 80%, and 100% across the plan period (the exact year alignment should be checked against the original table image).
3. Local mental-health ordinance implementation: the table lists 58 implementation sites (49 provinces and 9 cities). It notes that 6 of the 58 lacked mhGAP training and 23 of the 58 lacked an access site at the time recorded. These are source-period notes, not current counts.

**Goal 2 — Mental health conditions are identified, treated and prevented**

1. Suicide mortality rate per 100,000 population: baseline shown as 2017, value 3.2, with the table listing targets of 3.20, 3.17, 3.14 and 3.10 for the plan years 2020–2023. The source refers to WHO/World Health Statistics and the national survey as part of the baseline context; the denominator/formula field should be read carefully from the original table because the extracted text alignment is imperfect. The table notes baseline results due in July 2020 and that a rational target was to be determined in 2021.
2. Proportion of persons with psychosis, depression, anxiety disorders, substance use, and epilepsy who are using services: the table shows increases of 2%, 4%, 6% and 8% across 2020–2023. The baseline was to be informed by prevalence research / routine information systems as indicated in the table. The annual reporting frequency and the table’s stated assumptions must be preserved when this indicator is transcribed.

**Goal 3 — Persons affected by mental-health conditions are able to exercise the full range of human rights**

The goal and associated indicators appear in the M&E and scorecard tables, but row/column alignment and exact targets are not yet fully verified from the page images in this pass. Do not use a partial text extraction to construct a complete indicator list or claim that a target was met. Complete row-by-row transcription in Pass 5.

### Detailed outcome / indicator matrix

Later Annex 3 pages contain detailed scorecard-like matrices spanning promotion, governance, service delivery, and information/research. Visible examples include:

- a proxy indicator for the number and proportion of provinces, cities and municipalities implementing or trained in mhGAP, with source notes referring to a total of 1,259 localities (76 cities and 1,183 municipalities) in the table;
- a proxy indicator for localities accredited as Medicine Access Program (MAP) access sites, with the table listing “all provinces per region,” three municipalities and one city (until 2023), and a source note giving 362 access sites (64 cities, 32 provinces and 266 municipalities);
- a treatment-coverage indicator for selected mental, neurological and substance-use disorders in areas where affordable, quality services are offered, cross-referenced to Goal indicator 2.2, with sources such as iClinicSys, HOMIS, PhilHealth and DDAPTP reports;
- information-system and research-use indicators, including the percentage of barangay health stations, rural health units and hospitals using a standard information system, and the number of research outputs translated into policy, guidelines or guidance documents.

These examples are preserved as visible extracted entries, not certified as a complete list. Table headings, baselines, target-year alignment, agencies and means of verification must be verified visually before they are used as app facts or quiz answer keys.

### Reading an M&E indicator correctly

For each indicator, preserve the following separately when the source provides them:

1. Indicator name and scope/population.
2. Baseline year, value and source.
3. Target values by year (and 2030 target if shown).
4. Formula and numerator/denominator.
5. Data source / means of verification.
6. Responsible agency or agencies.
7. Reporting frequency.
8. Budget/fund source where specified.
9. Assumptions and risks.
10. Any source caveat, proxy label, cross-reference or TBD status.

Do not interpret a target as an observed result. A blank status cell, a planned survey, a “TBD” baseline, or a proxy indicator is not evidence of implementation or achievement.

The annex introduces performance standards and describes scorecards as electronic tools reflecting overall and stakeholder-/agency-specific accountabilities for delivering the results set out in the National Mental Health Strategic Plan 2019–2023.

6. Department of Labor and Employment (DOLE)

8. Department of Education (DepEd)
9. Commission on Higher Education (CHED)
10. Technical Education and Skills Development Authority (TESDA)
11. Department of Social Welfare and Development (DSWD)
12. Civil Service Commission (CSC)
13. Commission on Human Rights (CHR)

The detailed tables include indicators, baselines, targets by year (2020–2023), means of verification, objectively verifiable indicators, and responsible agencies. Some pages also contain proxy indicators and status/performance-standard bands. Exact row/column alignment and target values must be verified from the page images before being converted to searchable app data.

### Performance-standard legend visible in the source

The annex includes these bands:

- **0–50%:** No significant action initiated.
- **51–79%:** Significant action has been initiated and some progress has been made.
- **80–100%:** Significant progress has been made.

These are the source’s performance-standard descriptions. Do not recast them as a validated clinical scale, an independent evaluation, or proof that any agency achieved a particular band. A blank status cell is not a score.

### Scorecard study method

For each agency scorecard, identify:

- the result/accountability area assigned to that agency;
- each indicator and whether it is direct or marked as a proxy;
- baseline and annual targets;
- the specified means of verification and objectively verifiable evidence;
- the responsible agency shown in the table;
- the performance-standard legend and any status field;
- missing values, TBD values or table ambiguity that prevent interpretation.

Avoid assuming that an agency named as a data source is necessarily the same as the agency accountable for delivery. Preserve the table’s own role labels.
