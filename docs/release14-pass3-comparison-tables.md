## 1. Strategic hierarchy: goals vs pillars vs outcomes vs activities

| Concept | What it answers | How to recognize it | Do not confuse it with | Status / locator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vision** | What future does the Plan aspire to? | “Mental Health and Wellbeing for All Filipinos” | A measurable indicator or an achieved state | Source-supported; PDF p. 37 |
| **Mission** | What broad commitment guides the Plan? | Value, promote and protect the basic right of all Filipinos to mental health and wellbeing; provide comprehensive, integrated, accessible and quality programs and services | A single activity or target | Source-supported; PDF p. 37 |
| **Goals (3)** | What major changes are sought? | Valuing/promoting/protecting mental health; identifying/treating/preventing conditions; human rights of persons affected | The four implementation pillars | Source-supported; PDF pp. 37–40 |
| **Pillars (4)** | Through what broad strategic domains is work organized? | Promotion and Prevention; Leadership and Governance; Services; Information and Research | Goals or outcome count | Source-supported; PDF pp. 40–52 |
| **Outcomes (5)** | What result areas does the framework intend to advance? | Five outcome areas described in the Plan’s framework | A one-to-one mirror of the four pillars | Count source-supported; some diagram relationships verification-gated; PDF pp. 40–52 |
| **Outputs / key activities** | What deliverables or actions are planned? | More operational, implementable items under the strategic plan | Proof that a goal/outcome has already happened | Source-supported as plan structure; specific rows require their own page check |
| **Indicators / means of verification** | How is progress intended to be monitored? | Measures plus data/source or verification method | The result itself, or proof of causation | Source-supported as plan structure; row-level tables remain partly verification-gated |

**Contrast card**
- **Front:** “Four pillars” and “five outcomes” — what is the common trap?
- **Back:** Assuming the counts match or mapping one outcome to each pillar. The Plan identifies four pillars and five outcome areas; do not infer a one-to-one relationship without explicit source support.
- **Status:** Count source-supported; detailed diagram links verification-gated.

**Contrast card**
- **Front:** Is an indicator the same as an outcome?
- **Back:** No. An outcome is a desired result area; an indicator is a measure used to monitor progress toward a result. A planned indicator does not show that the outcome was achieved.
- **Status:** Learning explanation based on the Plan’s monitoring structure.

## 2. The three goals: keep the wording and intent distinct

| Goal | Source wording (retain for exact-recall tasks) | Study cue (learning explanation) | Common misreading |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Goal 1** | Mental health and wellbeing is valued, promoted, and protected. | Societal valuation, promotion and protection of mental health and wellbeing | Reducing the goal to clinical treatment alone |
| **Goal 2** | Mental health conditions are identified, treated and prevented. | Identification, treatment and prevention of conditions | Omitting prevention or treating this as the entire Plan |
| **Goal 3** | Persons affected by mental health conditions are able to exercise the full range of human rights. | Rights and the ability to exercise them | Treating rights as a side issue rather than an explicit goal |

**Source locator:** PDF p. 37; related goal/indicator tables PDF pp. 38–40. Exact punctuation follows the wording preserved in the existing source-mapped notes.

**Contrast card**
- **Front:** Which goal explicitly names the full range of human rights?
- **Back:** Goal 3. Do not replace it with a generic “improve services” formulation.

## 3. The four pillars: domain labels vs example interpretations

| Pillar (source label) | Broad study interpretation | Boundary to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| **Promotion and Prevention** | Work directed toward promoting mental health and preventing problems/conditions | Examples of specific programs must be tied to a source row or labeled as illustrative |
| **Leadership and Governance** | Leadership, coordination, policy and governance functions | Do not assume a particular agency is accountable for a specific target unless its source row is verified |
| **Services** | Mental-health service delivery and service-system development | Do not reduce services to specialist/tertiary care only; the Plan discusses balanced care |
| **Information and Research** | Information systems, evidence and research functions | Do not invent indicator definitions, data sources or reporting frequency |

**Status:** Pillar names source-supported; descriptions are learning explanations. See strategic framework notes, PDF pp. 40–52. Specific action examples and agency mappings require row-level citations.

**Contrast card**
- **Front:** “Services” vs “Information and Research” — what is the safest distinction?
- **Back:** Services concerns service-system provision; Information and Research concerns the information/evidence functions that can support planning and monitoring. This is a learning distinction, not a claim that the two operate independently.

## 4. Five guiding approaches/models: useful memory cues, not official definitions

| Approach named in the Plan | Brief learning cue | What not to claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Staging approach** | Needs and experiences may differ across stages | Do not treat the cue as a verbatim formal definition |
| **Biopsychosocial model** | Biological, psychological/developmental and social/environmental factors interact | Do not use it to infer a particular person’s case formulation from the policy document alone |
| **Balanced-care model** | Consider a mix of service platforms, including community and different levels of care | Do not claim the Plan’s diagram proves an achieved service distribution |
| **Rights-based approach** | Rights and freedoms of people requiring mental-health services are central | Do not reduce rights to optional good practice |
| **Recovery approach** | The person affected has a central role in defining problems and meaningful outcomes | Do not equate recovery with a single clinical endpoint |

**Status:** The Plan names these approaches; the short cues are learning explanations. Source locator: PDF pp. 40–52. Verify the relevant page image before scoring exact definition questions.

## 5. Policy instrument vs strategic plan vs implementation rules

| Instrument | Role in this study set | Best answer when asked… | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Republic Act No. 11036 (Mental Health Act)** | Statutory policy/legal framework summarized in Annex 1 | “What is the Act?” or “What broad legal framework does the Plan reference?” | Do not invent section numbers or legal quotations from the unverified/scanned text. Consult the official law for legal interpretation. |
| **Implementing Rules and Regulations (IRR)** | Rules that operationalize the Act; included as Annex 2 | “What document contains implementing rules?” | Annex 2 pages are scanned and detailed text is not fully verified in this pass. Avoid detailed rule/section questions until page-image review. |
| **National Mental Health Strategic Plan 2019–2023** | Time-bounded strategy/implementation plan with goals, pillars, outcomes, activities, targets, indicators and responsibilities | “How was implementation intended to be organized and monitored during 2019–2023?” | It is not itself the full text of the Act, the IRR, or a patient-level clinical guideline. |
| **Monitoring and Evaluation (M&E) Plan / Annex 3** | Monitoring structure, indicators, means of verification and responsibilities as set out in the Plan | “How did the Plan intend to monitor progress?” | Detailed row alignment is still partly verification-gated; do not fabricate current performance. |

**Contrast card**
- **Front:** Is the Strategic Plan interchangeable with RA 11036 or its IRR?
- **Back:** No. The Act is the law; the IRR provides implementing rules; the 2019–2023 Strategic Plan organizes a time-bounded strategy and its intended implementation/monitoring. This is a high-level learning distinction, not legal advice.

**Status:** High-level roles are source-supported in the existing notes. Specific legal sections, quotations and IRR rules remain verification-gated. Annex 1 PDF pp. 53–73; Annex 2 and 3 ranges/page-label differences are recorded in Release 13 Pass 4.

## 6. Plan targets vs results vs present-day status

| Term | Meaning in revision questions | Safe interpretation | Unsafe inference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Target** | Intended level or milestone for the 2019–2023 planning period | A planned benchmark | “The target was achieved” without outcome data |
| **Indicator** | A measure selected to monitor progress | Defines what would be measured | Treating the indicator as the result itself |
| **Means of verification / data source** | Evidence source intended to support monitoring | Indicates where evidence should come from | Assuming data are complete, valid or currently available |
| **Reported result / performance value** | An observed value for a defined period, if one is provided | Interpret only with its period, denominator, definition and source | Assuming every target table includes a result |
| **Current status after 2023** | Present-day implementation or outcomes | Requires current evidence outside this historical plan | Inferring current performance from the 2019–2023 plan alone |

**Contrast card**
- **Front:** A plan lists a 2023 target. Does that establish achievement?
- **Back:** No. A target documents intended performance. Achievement requires evidence of actual results for the relevant period.

**Status:** Core source-literacy rule. Several detailed M&E/scorecard rows remain verification-gated. Historical framing: all topics.

## 9. Historical situation data vs current estimates

| Source-period statistic/context | How to use it in an answer | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Statistics in the Plan’s situation analysis | Attribute the number to the Plan and its cited period/source | Presenting it as a current Philippine estimate without newer evidence |
| Workforce, facility or service-use figures | Preserve denominator, unit, geography and source period when available | Comparing values with newer data as if definitions and coverage are identical |
| Policy or service gaps described in the Plan | State that these were gaps identified in the historical planning context | Claiming they remain unchanged or have been resolved without current evidence |

## 11. Mixed contrast drill (candidate items for later question-authoring)

These are draft prompts for Pass 4. Do not automatically score any verification-gated detail.

1. **Four pillars or three goals?** Recall three goals as desired changes and four pillars as strategic domains.
2. **Outcome or indicator?** Identify the desired result area versus the measure used to monitor progress.
3. **Act, IRR or Plan?** Match statute, implementing rules, and time-bounded strategic implementation plan.
4. **Target or achieved result?** Require actual performance evidence before claiming achievement.
5. **Proposed budget or actual expenditure?** The Annex 4 values are proposed historical budget amounts.
6. **No data or zero performance?** The Annex 5 legend treats these as distinct categories.
7. **Historical situation statistic or current estimate?** Attribute the statistic to its source period unless current evidence is available.
8. **Source statement or learning cue?** Exact source labels and the teaching mnemonic must not be presented as interchangeable quotations.
